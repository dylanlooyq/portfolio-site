Accelerating Tabular Reconciliation

Ten ways to compare two huge tables, measured on time, memory, CPU and code.

A month-end reconciliation sounds simple until the files reach millions of rows and the spreadsheet stops opening. I treated it as a research question: for a row-by-row comparison of two large tables, which execution strategy gives the shortest time to an answer, and what does it cost?

I benchmarked ten methods (seven from an earlier study, three new) on 7M-row Parquet files, then scaled up to 56M. Each run is a fresh process, and every answer is checked against ground truth recorded by the data generator.

What I found

  • Fastest is not the cheapest. Polars (Vectorized) took 0.56 s but peaked at 2.4 GB. Tuned PyArrow took 1.2 s with 4.8× less memory.
  • Memory decides what scales. At 56M rows the winner changed: parallel PyArrow finished in 7.8 s while the 7M-row champion could not run at all.
  • Skipping beats speeding up. Comparing raw Parquet row-group bytes and decoding only what differs is near-instant on mostly unchanged files, and gains nothing once every group is touched.
  • Parallelism has a price. The parallel method kept 8.4 cores busy for 3.3× the CPU of the fastest, and was slower at 7M rows.
  • Hand-written is not automatically faster. A 67-line Numba kernel matched a 36-line PyArrow version.

How it was run

  • Workload: two 7M-row, 12-column Parquet files with 200 altered rows, scaled from 1M to 56M.
  • Fair fight: a fresh process per run, seven interleaved repeats, and a memory guard that records what cannot finish.
  • Correct by construction: every method is tested against the changed rows the generator recorded.
